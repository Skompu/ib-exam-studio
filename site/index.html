Syllabus coverage

The question bank follows the AA syllabus.

Original Paper 1, Paper 2, and HL Paper 3 questions are dealt from the five strands of Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches. HL and SL share the core. HL keeps the extra topics, including Paper 3. Every paper is new values, with a mark scheme written for that paper.

Number and algebra

  • Arithmetic and geometric sequences and series, including infinite geometric series
  • The binomial theorem, its general term, and the general expansion
  • Logarithms and exponential equations
  • Quadratics, the discriminant, sum and product of roots, remainder and factor theorems
  • Partial fractions (HL) and linear systems with a parameter (HL)
  • Proof by a direct argument or by contradiction
  • HL: induction, and complex numbers — polar form, Argand diagram, conjugate roots, de Moivre, loci

Functions

  • Composition, domain, and inverse
  • Transformations and the modulus
  • Rational functions and asymptotes
  • Piecewise functions
  • Logarithmic functions and their inverses
  • Exponential and logarithmic models

Geometry and trigonometry

  • Exact trigonometric equations
  • Sine rule, double angles, compound angles, and identities
  • The ambiguous case, and the form R sin(x + α)
  • Secant, cosecant, cotangent, and inverse trigonometric functions
  • Radian measure, sectors, chords, and segments
  • HL: vectors — skew lines, the cross product, a line and a plane, the angle between two planes

Statistics and probability

  • Conditional probability
  • Discrete and continuous random variables, density, median, and expectation
  • Binomial distribution and counting principles (HL: permutations & combinations)
  • The normal distribution and the inverse normal
  • Linear regression and box plots
  • Pearson's r, box plots with IQR outliers, and bivariate regression

Calculus

  • Differentiation, stationary points, and points of inflection
  • Tangents, normals, implicit differentiation, and related rates
  • Definite integrals and area, including a numerical area on a GDC
  • Integration by parts and by substitution
  • Volumes of revolution and L'Hôpital's rule
  • Kinematics in a straight line
  • HL: differential equations (separable, homogeneous, integrating factor, logistic) and Maclaurin series

Paper 3 and longer questions

  • HL Paper 3 investigations: a geometric series, Maclaurin error, a recurrence and its closed form, powers of sine, and powers on the unit circle
  • A full 60-minute HL Paper 3, or a shorter topic probe
  • Section B models: a cubic, cooling, drug clearance, a sinusoid, an exponential-linear curve, a cylinder of fixed volume, linked sequences, or a normal model with a binomial count

A working bank, grouped the way a teacher reads the syllabus.

It is not a line-by-line copy of every syllabus bullet. SL papers leave out complex numbers, induction, Maclaurin series, differential equations, vectors, and Paper 3.

Yes. Export a student paper and a matching mark scheme as PDFs, or download the .tex source to edit the header in Overleaf. Exam Booklet leaves ruled working space. Worksheet packs the same paper into fewer pages.

What do Challenging and Issued do?

Challenging keeps the same topics and the same marks as Standard. A question may ask for one extra step, or a less obvious case. Every group stays on that setting. Different numbers between groups come from the Groups slider, not from this switch.

Issued is the archive of papers you saved with “Save paper to school archive”. Open one to read every group and reprint the student PDF. Export Archive downloads a JSON copy of those papers, plus class lists, blocked topics, and questions saved for later.

Does the question bank stay still?

New question archetypes are added weekly across the core AA topics, so a later paper is not a reprint of the one you dealt last month.
